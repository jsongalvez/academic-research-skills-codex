# Literary MT Evidence Synthesis

## Procedure

1. Read current project state, claim register, and relevant evidence records.
2. State the decision or question. Exclude evidence that does not bear on it.
3. Group findings by proposition, not by publication order.
4. Separate direct evidence from indirect evidence; source findings from interpretations; independent replication from self-report; reader outcomes from automatic metrics.
5. Map convergence, disagreement, missing evidence, and plausible causes (language, genre, sample, evaluator expertise, task design, model revision, or context).
6. State the strongest conclusion justified, then state what remains unknown.
7. Convert the synthesis into a decision, bounded next question, or falsifiable experiment.
8. Update existing project records in place.

## Literary MT priorities

- Source fidelity before stylistic polish.
- Reader preference only among translations that pass a source-grounded fidelity gate.
- Preserve narrative voice, character identity, relationships, viewpoint, register, figurative language, and terminology.
- Test consistency across separated passages and chapters, not only adjacent sentences.
- Count human review/correction burden and total cost.

BLEU, chrF, COMET, and LLM-judge scores are diagnostic unless validated against the reader outcome of interest. Do not allow an aggregate score to hide severe mistranslations, omissions, changed relationships, identity drift, or voice collapse.

A useful evaluation sequence is:
1. source-grounded fidelity gate;
2. blind reader preference among fidelity-acceptable outputs;
3. consistency and literary diagnostics;
4. correction time and total cost.

## Required synthesis

- **Question / decision**
- **Evidence supporting**
- **Evidence against or qualifying**
- **What is established**
- **What is not established**
- **Assumptions**
- **Decision implication**
- **Cheapest useful falsification test**

Preserve negative findings. If evidence cannot support a choice, say so.
